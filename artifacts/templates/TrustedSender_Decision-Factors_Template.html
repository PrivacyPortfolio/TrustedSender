TrustedSender Decision-Factors

(How I Decide Whether a Sender Is Trusted)

 

I classify senders using a zero trust model that evaluates technical, behavioral, and infrastructure signals.
A sender is not “trusted” because of who they claim to be — they are trusted because of how their email behaves.
Below are the decision factors I use.

1. TLS Negotiation & Cipher Strength

decision-factor-1a: whether the sender uses modern encryption

decision-factor-1b: whether they support TLS 1.3

decision-factor-1c: whether they fall back to insecure ciphers

decision-factor-1d: whether they use deprecated key exchange

decision-factor-1e: whether they downgrade under load

2. MTA STS & TLS Reporting

decision-factor-2a: Whether the sender publishes an MTA STS policy

decision-factor-2b: Whether the policy is “enforce”, “testing”, or “none”

decision-factor-2c: Whether TLSRPT reports are valid

decision-factor-2d: Whether the domain supports DANE (rare but strong)

3. ARC (Authenticated Received Chain) Behavior

decision-factor-3a: ARC Seal presence

decision-factor-3b: ARC Message Signature presence

decision-factor-3c: ARC chain validity

decision-factor-3d: Whether ARC breaks across vendors

4. Reverse DNS & PTR Hygiene

decision-factor-4a: PTR record validity

decision-factor-4b: PTR → A record consistency

decision-factor-4c: Whether PTR matches the sending domain

decision-factor-4d: Whether PTR reveals hidden vendors

5. Mail Server Reputation & Historical Behavior

decision-factor-5a: Spamhaus listings

Why It Matters

Authentication: If your message cannot prove who you are, your message will not be trusted.

Routing: If your message is routed through multiple external entities, confidentiality may be compromised.

Behavioral Intent: If your message collects information about me which I haven't consented to, and isn't disclosed in response to a RIGHT TO KNOW request, your message will not be trusted.

TrustedSender Decision-Outcomes

Based on these factors, I classify your message as:

  • TrustedSender — full identity assurance + accountability
  • SponsoredSender — invited and vouched for by a TrustedSender
  • GuestSender — invited by a TrustedSender for a temporary time window
  • VisitingSender — unregistered, identifiable Sender
  • UnknownSender — unregistered, anonymous Sender
  • UnTrustedSender — unregistered, identifiable Sender or terminated TrustedSender
  • Marketing Sender — no PHI/SPI content
  • PHI Sender — no Marketing parties
  • Dual Use Sender — mixes Marketing and PHI in email messages

This classification determines whether your message is delivered, blocked, quarantined, escalated, or used as forensic evidence.

My goal is not to block communication.
My goal is to protect myself from non-responsive Senders that collect information about me without my consent.
